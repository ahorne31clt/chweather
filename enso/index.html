WHY THEY DIFFER

The background ocean matters.

RONI removes the tropical-mean SST anomaly from Niño 3.4 and rescales the difference. ONI is the traditional Niño 3.4 anomaly index. Neither value by itself describes all of the atmosphere’s response.

EL NIÑO / TYPICAL WINTER TENDENCY

A more active southern storm track.

El Niño winters tend to bring wetter conditions to North Carolina, with opportunities for coastal lows. That is a seasonal tendency, not a prediction for every storm.

LA NIÑA / TYPICAL WINTER TENDENCY

A different route for storms.

La Niña can favor a storm track farther north through the Ohio Valley, with a tendency toward less precipitation in North Carolina. Individual winters can depart from that pattern.

SNOW & ICE

Moisture needs cold air.

A wet winter does not automatically mean a snowy one. Storm track, cold-air supply, and temperatures through the atmosphere must align. ENSO alone cannot tell us Charlotte’s snowfall total.

NEUTRAL & OTHER INFLUENCES

The Pacific is one ingredient.

Neutral means neither warm nor cool ENSO conditions dominate. It does not mean average weather. Other circulation patterns and shorter-term variability still matter.

WHAT THE PHASE MEANS

Ocean and atmosphere,
working together.

El Niño and La Niña describe coupled patterns in the tropical Pacific. Warm or cool ocean anomalies are one part of the picture; trade winds, convection and other atmospheric signals also matter.

NOAA transitioned to operational RONI monitoring in February 2026. Historical threshold-based episodes involve persistence across at least five overlapping seasons; a single value above or below a threshold is not an official declaration.
