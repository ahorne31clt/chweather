The Pacific, over time.

RONI / relative indexONI / traditional indexUnit: °C

Select a season to read its indices.

RONI adjusts Niño 3.4 anomalies for tropical-mean ocean warming and rescales the result. ONI and RONI are distinct measures; both use overlapping three-month periods. The ±0.5°C lines are reference thresholds, not an automatic declaration of El Niño or La Niña. Recent estimates may change.

WHAT THE PHASE MEANS

Ocean and atmosphere,
working together.

El Niño and La Niña describe coupled patterns in the tropical Pacific. Warm or cool ocean anomalies are one part of the picture; trade winds, convection and other atmospheric signals also matter.

NOAA transitioned to operational RONI monitoring in February 2026. Historical threshold-based episodes involve persistence across at least five overlapping seasons; a single value above or below a threshold is not an official declaration. This tracker displays CPC’s published alert status directly.

PUTTING IT TO WORK

Seasonal context.
Local decisions.

ENSO can influence seasonal climate odds, but does not determine every storm, cold spell or rainfall event. Strong index values do not guarantee a particular local impact.

Use CPC’s temperature and precipitation outlooks for seasonal context, then your local NWS forecast for the weather ahead. This tracker’s monthly discussion, three-month indices, weekly observations and forecast graphic update on different schedules.
