CH WEATHER / SATELLITE GUIDE

See the atmosphere.

GeoColor: approximate true color by day, a multispectral infrared view at night. Nighttime city lights are a static orientation layer, not current observations. Credit: CIRA / NOAA.

Clean infrared: emitted radiation near 10.3 µm, useful day and night. Brightness temperature often reflects cloud-top temperature; it is not surface air temperature. Read the color scale printed on NOAA’s image.

Water vapor: upper, mid- and lower-tropospheric moisture patterns. These channels have broad, changing sensitivities; they do not measure surface humidity or rainfall.

Red visible: reflected sunlight; dark at night. Air Mass and cloud-microphysics RGBs combine channels in false color. Sandwich imagery combines visible texture and infrared information.

Timing: each frame shows its observation time from NOAA’s timestamped filename. CONUS commonly updates every five minutes; regional/full-disk products commonly every ten minutes. Actual gaps and processing delays vary. The viewer refreshes the available frame list every five minutes when enabled and the tab is visible; it does not generate new scans. Imagery older than one hour is labeled as delayed.

Controls: drag to pan after zooming; Fit resets the view. Left/right arrows step frames, Home/End jump to the first/latest, and Space plays or pauses when focus is outside a control. Image opens the original timestamped NOAA image. Regional East views switch to CONUS when choosing West.

NOAA imagery is displayed unchanged, with source legends and boundaries retained. Zoom changes display size only. These images do not supply pixel-temperature readouts, radar rain rates, or warnings.
